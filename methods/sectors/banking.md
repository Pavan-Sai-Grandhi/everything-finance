# Banking and NBFCs

## Sub-groups

Read these separately, because each one has its own problem [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@01:45]:

- Large private banks.
- PSU banks.
- Small finance banks.
- NBFCs (vehicle, gold, consumer, microfinance).
- Housing finance.

## KPIs

- **NIM.** Interest earned minus interest paid, over assets. It is the lender's gross margin, with depositors' money as the raw material [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@03:59]. As a rule of thumb, above 3.5% is strong for a private bank and about 3% is the PSU norm [varsity-sa].
- **CASA ratio.** Above 40% means a cheap deposit franchise [varsity-sa]. CASA falls when savers move money to markets. Banks then raise term deposits at higher rates, which pushes up the cost of funds and compresses NIM [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@04:22].
- **Credit-to-deposit (CD) ratio.** A bank far above the regulator's comfort level has to grow deposits faster than loans or slow its lending. That caps growth for a year or two [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@12:58].
- **Asset quality.** The direction of GNPA and NNPA matters more than their level, and GNPA under 3% is comfortable [varsity-sa]. Credit cost is the profit set aside against bad loans [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@02:12]. In vehicle finance, NPAs can look inflated by late payments that are eventually recovered [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@34:10].
- **ROA and ROE.** Read ROE without a one-off deferred-tax-asset boost [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@40:03].
- **CAR.** Capital adequacy should sit above the regulatory minimum with a buffer [varsity-sa].
- **Valuation.** Value a lender on P/B, set against its own history and its peers, not on P/E [varsity-sa].

## Swing factors to check

- **Rate cycle and book type.** On a fixed-rate loan book, NIM gets squeezed when rates rise and widens when rates fall. On a floating-rate book, both sides reprice [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@37:33]. Check the bank's own disclosed mix.
- **Regulator action.** RBI circulars on CD ratio, unsecured lending, onboarding bans and risk weights can de-rate a name or the whole sector. Cite the circular itself.
- **Size.** Growth gets harder past a certain book size. For a housing financier, falling below 20 to 25% growth raises de-rating risk [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@34:43].

## What breaks the story

- Gross NPAs turning up. The banking cycle runs while GNPA keeps falling, and bull markets and
  credit cycles tend to end together [soic-sectors:3reNGUatnUE@03:47] [soic-sectors:3reNGUatnUE@14:43].

- A credit event, or RBI action on an NBFC. Microfinance and unsecured lending are hit first [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@36:02].
- Growth bought by diluting return ratios. Compare loan growth with the ROA trend [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@26:15].
- Recent listings have no cycle history to judge them against [soic-sectors:3RhFffI1WK0@39:55].
