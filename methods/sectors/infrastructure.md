## The economics

These are toll-road businesses. The asset is built once, its replacement cost rises with
inflation, and income grows with traffic, so the return on historical cost climbs
[soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@00:12]. Start from first principles: list how the operator makes money,
stream by stream [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@05:40].

| Stream | Character |
|---|---|
| Regulated (airport aero charges, tariffs) | Set by the regulator to allow a capped return on equity. The cap makes growth predictable, not high [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@07:01] [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@07:44]. Cite AERA's (or the sector regulator's) tariff order |
| Unregulated (retail, duty free, F&B, parking, lounges) | Higher margin, with no cap. This is where operators grow [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@07:55] |
| Land monetisation | Leasing land around the asset for hotels, offices and logistics [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@08:46] |

Concessionaires and tenants (F&B and lounge operators) are a proxy play on the same traffic. Their
risk is the operator taking the business in-house [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@24:39].

## Ports

The four models differ [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@10:04]:

- **Landlord.** The authority owns the land and earns rent and fees.
- **Terminal operator.** A private firm runs the berths.
- **Service.** The port does everything.
- **Tool.** The port owns the equipment and a private operator uses it.

Major ports are central, and non-major ports are state or private
[soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@11:13]. Private operators taking share from government ports is the
listed story. Geography sets the cargo mix:

- The east coast handles more coal and ore.
- The west coast handles more containers and petroleum.

Containers earn more per tonne and signal finished-goods trade
[soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@22:45] [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@23:30].

The port is the bottleneck of the logistics chain. Warehousing, rail and trucking downstream face
heavy competition, so an operator that integrates them cuts out middlemen
[soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@15:30].

Entry barriers [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@01:06] [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@12:40]:

- ₹10,000 crore or more of capital.
- 25 to 35 year concessions.
- Multiple clearances.
- A limited number of suitable sites.

Inflation raises the cost of replicating the asset [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@13:04]. Cite
Sagarmala, Gati Shakti and the cargo statistics from the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and
Waterways [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@14:10].

## KPIs

- **Traffic growth** (passengers, cargo, vessel calls), from the authority's statistics: DGCA for
  air, the Ministry of Ports for ports [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@06:00].
- **Revenue mix** between regulated and unregulated streams, and its trend.
- **Cash conversion.** CFO to PAT and CFO to EBITDA. An operator that sets payment terms has no
  working-capital drag [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@29:25].
- **Captive volume.** The share of cargo from the operator's own group against third parties
  [soic-sectors:3nmtnVb7pJk@33:09].
- **Debt, interest cover and cash flow.** These assets are built on debt, so look past the growth
  story to the balance sheet [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@29:27].

## What breaks the story

- Concession expiry. Read each concession's end date [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@29:03].
- High debt against operating profit.
- Government interference in pricing.
- A slowdown in traffic [soic-sectors:snogVxecaVs@28:38].
