# Term life — best-practice reference

Pure protection for dependents.

## Interview — what to ask (one at a time)

Age · annual income · dependents + their ages · outstanding loans (home/auto/personal) · existing life cover · smoker/tobacco · target retirement age. For the liability-and-goals calculation, also ask annual family expenses, education goals and years of support. A positive support horizon needs an expense figure; missing expenses are a gap, not zero. Also useful: existing liquid corpus (nets down the goals method), any known health conditions (affects insurability + premium honesty).

## Cover sizing (feeds `term_cover_need`)

With all household and scenario inputs supplied explicitly, compare:
- **Income replacement scenario**: a chosen income multiple × annual income. Explain why that multiple fits; the calculator does not pick it.
- **Liability + goals**: outstanding loans + children's education corpus + (annual family expenses × years to youngest dependent's independence) − existing liquid corpus.

The calculator takes the larger of the two, then subtracts existing cover (floored at zero). It ignores inflation and discounting, so say so, and check that the liquid corpus is really available for these goals. Set the policy term from the household's support horizon, not a default retirement age.

## Scoring checklist (weighted — score each candidate, higher = better)

| Criterion | Weight | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Claim settlement ratio (IRDAI)** | high | high and stable across years by count (~97%+ is a common rule of thumb, not an IRDAI threshold), cross-checked with **amount-settlement ratio** (count alone hides big-ticket rejections); cite the IRDAI annual report year |
| **Solvency ratio** | high | comfortably above IRDAI's 1.5 floor |
| **Premium for the cover** | high | competitive per ₹1 Cr for the age/term (band in § Sizing sanity) |
| **Claim experience / repudiation reputation** | med | few disputed-rejection complaints; ombudsman record clean |
| **Premium-paying-term flexibility** | med | regular-pay usually best value; limited-pay only if it genuinely fits cashflow |
| **Rider quality** | med | see below — worth-taking vs gimmick |
| **Insurer stability / vintage** | low | established book, no solvency scares |

## Riders — worth it vs gimmick

- **Worth considering:** accidental death/disability, **waiver-of-premium** (premiums waived if you're disabled), critical-illness only if you lack separate cover.
- **Usually gimmicks:** **return-of-premium** (a bad FD bolted on — inflates premium massively for near-zero real return), income-payout variants that just re-package the sum assured.

## Red flags

Each criterion above, failed, is a flag; these are the sales-behaviour ones the table can't hold:

- Recommending an endowment/ULIP/"guaranteed return" plan when the need is pure protection — the classic mis-sell.
- Pressure to under-declare smoking or a condition to lower premium — that's the future claim rejection.
- Very long premium-paying-term or cover-till-99 framed as "value".
- Medical test waived on a large cover for an older applicant — non-medical underwriting raises later-repudiation risk.

## Sizing sanity (unverified rule of thumb)

A commonly quoted band of about ₹12–18k/yr per ₹1 Cr for a healthy 30-something, rising with age and for smokers, has no source. Use it only to spot an outlier quote. Judge price from real quotes for the same age, term and riders, and never cite the band as evidence.
