## KPIs

- **EBITDA per tonne.** This is the main metric. Pipe makers are converters: raw material is
  about 90% of sales value, so the percentage margin is thin. Read earnings per tonne
  instead [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@38:23] [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@41:07].
- **Integration.** An integrated maker (ore to sponge iron to billet to strip to pipe) has
  process stages whose earnings swing separately; one weak stage is covered by the others
  [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@48:11] [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@1:06:20].
- **Capacity being added**, against demand [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@38:27].

## Swing factors to check

- Steel coil and PVC resin prices, and the import share of resin [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@36:24].
- Government water and pipeline programmes, cited from the ministry.

## Who wins

- Low-cost producers who still sell at a premium, and brands that buy cheaper and sell dearer.
- Integrated players.
- Niche players.
- Makers with a wide range, so dealers stock them.
- Geographically spread makers. Freight limits most pipe to a few hundred kilometres from the
  plant [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@1:01:53] [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@1:08:08].
- Above all, makers that execute what they announce [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@1:11:30].

A converter's downturn usually lasts one or two quarters of dealer destocking and inventory loss.
A primary producer stays hit until prices recover [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@42:42].

## What breaks the story

Capacity added without demand pushes EBITDA per tonne down and can trap a leveraged maker in
debt [soic-sectors:2o-FV9b-yJw@38:54].
