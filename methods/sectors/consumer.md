A consumption cycle ends when valuations have stretched (P/E of 100 or more) and penetration of the main categories leaves little growth [soic-sectors:ICz5ejNhJFc@03:05]. Read the multiple against penetration headroom before calling a consumer name a compounder.

High-ROCE consumer categories draw well-funded entrants (a conglomerate in paints, in cola, in quick commerce). The leader's multiple de-rates even while demand grows, so name the entrants and their capacity [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@07:27] [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@10:54].

## FMCG

Read a consumer company through three lenses [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@00:50]:

1. **Category penetration.** A near-saturated category (toothpaste) grows with population. An
   under-penetrated one (energy drinks) has headroom. Cite a penetration figure, not an
   impression [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@00:56].
2. **Volume against value.** Growth from selling more units is what the market rewards. Growth
   from price alone fades [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@01:36] [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@02:16].
3. **Premiumisation.** In a saturated category, growth comes from customers trading up, which
   also lifts margin [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@02:26].

The companies sort into types, each with its own risk [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@03:38]:

| Type | What it is | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Market leader | Double-digit volume growth from a model that has cracked Indian demand | Valuation [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@34:25] |
| Mean reversion | A slow, saturated category with margins hurt by input costs | Input prices staying high; watch new-product volume [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@22:10] |
| Proxy (packaging, glass) | Sells to the consumer companies | Raw-material swings, the end industry slowing, a packaging ban [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@12:23] |
| Premiumisation | Trading-up within a category | Downtrading in a slowdown |
| Exporter | Consumer products sold abroad | Trade barriers and freight [soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@38:12] |

Watch value migrating within a category, such as hair oil users moving to gels
[soic-sectors:SwmOLZ7Bp3g@22:43]. Rural against urban commentary, and gross margin against input
costs, come from the concalls [plugin].

## Dairy

- **Regional by nature.** Milk spoils without chilling, so procurement, chilling and distribution
  each work within a short radius, and expansion goes state by state over years
  [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@00:21] [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@27:42]. Farmer trust in prompt
  payment decides who procures [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@01:28].
- **Product mix is the story.** Liquid milk earns a thin margin, while curd, paneer, butter,
  ice cream and whey earn several times more. The winners move their mix from liquid milk to
  value-added products, which also makes margins less cyclical
  [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@06:41] [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@25:02].
- **The margin cycle is shallow.** Margins move one to three points, not into losses. The
  drivers are [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@19:19] [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@21:35]:
  - the flush season's milk supply;
  - the monsoon;
  - cattle disease;
  - global skimmed-milk-powder prices;
  - cost pass-through that lags by one or two quarters [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@23:07].
- **Formalisation.** A large unorganised share is moving to packaged brands, and quick commerce
  sells only packaged milk [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@51:40].
- **Compare peers on** gross margin, value-added share of sales, CFO to EBITDA and working-capital
  days [soic-sectors:0i_mVwvtXuE@55:21].

## Jewellery

- **Value migration** from unorganised to organised jewellers is the story. GST, compulsory BIS
  hallmarking and anti-money-laundering rules drive it, so cite each from its authority. Several
  organised players can win at once [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@03:40] [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@08:19]
  [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@10:22].
- **Studded ratio** (diamond and gemstone share) drives margin. Gold carries no pricing premium
  [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@14:03] [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@22:54].
- **Hedging.** Gold metal loans, forward contracts and customer gold schemes remove exposure to
  the gold price. Gold metal loans sit in inventory and make operating cash flow look negative,
  so adjust for them before calling cash flow weak [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@16:41]
  [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@33:19].
- **Regions and seasons.** The South buys mostly plain gold year-round, while the North and West
  buy more studded jewellery. The wedding quarter (October to December) is the strongest
  [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@13:10] [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@06:40].
- **Risks.**
  - Customs duty on gold.
  - A sharp gold-price rise, which cuts volumes for a quarter or two.
  - Fast expansion into new regions, which shows in same-store growth and ROCE
    [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@31:00].
  - Lab-grown diamonds, which threaten high-studded, high-ticket jewellers more than plain-gold
    ones [soic-sectors:J6tTMG2FxCA@46:21].

## Consumer durables (room ACs as the worked case)

- **Penetration** is low, at a single-digit share of households, against much higher shares in
  peer economies. That keeps the long-run trend intact through bad seasons. Cite the household
  survey or industry body [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@03:40].
- **Seasonality.** Summer quarters carry close to half the year's sales. A mild summer leaves
  stock with dealers and cuts the makers' next orders [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@01:50]
  [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@14:41].
- **Brand against contract manufacturer.**
  - A brand's own factory would run only a few months a year.
  - A contract maker pools many brands, buys components at scale and spreads labour cost.
  - So the contract layer persists even as brands grow [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@12:33].
- **Components** are largely imported. The component PLI and technology joint ventures pull
  them onshore. Cite the scheme from the ministry [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@15:04].
- **Track:**
  - Volume growth and share, with margin beside it, because share bought at a thin margin
    leaves poor returns [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@14:35].
  - Brands insourcing manufacturing.
  - Copper and freight costs [soic-sectors:BtlIHcvhYZg@39:55].

## Automobiles [varsity-sa]

- Monthly volume data, released around the 1st of each month, is the highest-frequency signal in the market. Cite SIAM or the company release.
- Realisation per vehicle, EBITDA margin, and inventory days at dealers.
- Sub-segments rotate separately:
  - Two-wheelers are a rural proxy.
  - Passenger vehicles are an urban proxy.
  - Commercial vehicles are a capex and economy proxy.
  - Tractors follow the monsoon.

## Hotels [varsity-sa]

- ARR (average room rate) and occupancy.
- RevPAR = ARR × occupancy.
- Operating leverage is extreme.
