## Sub-groups

| Group | Economics |
|---|---|

| Wires (housing, retail) | Low barrier to entry. Distribution is the moat, so any entrant with a dealer network can come in [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@21:26] |

Copper and aluminium are the raw material, and a producer integrated into copper smelting has a
cost edge [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@21:13] [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@22:56].

## KPIs

- **Asset turn on gross block.** It is high, about 5 to 7x sales, so a small capex buys a lot of
  sales capacity [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@15:51].
- **Operating margin** against its own lifetime high. Ask whether a margin already at its peak can
  expand further [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@24:59].
- **ROCE** by company, and whether the industry's ROCE is being diluted
  [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@16:36].

## Swing factors to check

- **Capacity added against demand.** Sum the announced capex of listed players and entrants
  (cement, metals and conglomerate groups have entered), times the asset turn. Compare that with
  industry demand grown at its realised rate [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@18:12]
  [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@20:30].
- **Government capex pace.** Cite the budget documents or the ministry, not a headline
  [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@25:10].

## What breaks the story

- Capacity growing faster than demand while government spending slows. That leads to a price war
  [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@25:23].
- Multiples reverting to their mean even as earnings grow [soic-sectors:3S7Ky_bHf8Q@27:13].
